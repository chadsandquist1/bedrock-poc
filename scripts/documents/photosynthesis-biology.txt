Photosynthesis: The Foundation of Life on Earth

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy. This process is fundamental to life on Earth as it produces oxygen and serves as the primary source of energy for nearly all ecosystems.

The Basic Equation:
6CO2 + 6H2O + Light Energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2

In simple terms, photosynthesis is the process by which plants convert sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into glucose and oxygen.

Key Components:
- Sunlight: Provides the energy needed to drive the reaction
- Water (H2O): Absorbed through the roots and transported to leaves
- Carbon Dioxide (CO2): Absorbed from the air through tiny pores called stomata
- Chlorophyll: The green pigment in leaves that captures light energy

Products:
- Glucose: Used by the plant for energy and growth
- Oxygen: Released into the atmosphere as a byproduct

Photosynthesis occurs primarily in the leaves of plants, specifically in organelles called chloroplasts. The process is essential for maintaining atmospheric oxygen levels and removing carbon dioxide from the air.
