States of Matter: A Physics Overview

Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space. All matter exists in different states depending on temperature and pressure conditions.

The Three Basic States of Matter:

1. SOLID
- Particles are tightly packed in a fixed arrangement
- Has a definite shape and volume
- Particles vibrate in place but don't move freely
- Examples: ice, rock, wood, metal

2. LIQUID
- Particles are close together but can move around each other
- Has a definite volume but takes the shape of its container
- Flows and can be poured
- Examples: water, milk, oil, juice

3. GAS
- Particles are spread far apart and move freely
- Has no definite shape or volume
- Expands to fill its container
- Examples: air, oxygen, helium, water vapor

The three states of matter are solid, liquid, and gas. Matter can change from one state to another through processes like melting, freezing, evaporation, and condensation.

Additional States:
Scientists have also identified other states of matter including plasma (found in stars and lightning) and Bose-Einstein condensate (achieved at extremely low temperatures).
